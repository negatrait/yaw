### Context & Problem Statement

Human knowledge bases and Obsidian markdown vaults exhibit high lexical entropy, colloquial phrasing, and rampant lexical polysemy:
1. **Polysemy & Ambiguity:** A surface mention such as *"Transformer"* may refer to an attention-based neural network architecture (`concepts:transformer-neural-network`) or an electrical induction apparatus (`concepts:electrical-transformer`). Similarly, *"Mercury"* may refer to a chemical element (`entities:mercury-element`) or a celestial body (`entities:mercury-planet`).
2. **The Fallacy of Winner-Take-All Discretization:** Traditional entity linking systems employ greedy winner-take-all discretization at extraction time:
   $$e^* = \arg\max_{e \in \text{cands}(a)} P(e \mid a, \text{context})$$
   When an entity is extracted from an isolated bullet point or short paragraph, local context is often insufficient to resolve ambiguity reliably. Forcing a discrete assignment at ingestion time commits hard epistemic errors irreversibly to the database, polluting downstream graph conductances.
3. **The Overhead of Pairwise LLM Adjudication:** Calling an LLM to disambiguate every polysemous candidate during continuous ingestion introduces $\mathcal{O}(N \times M)$ latency, non-deterministic stalling, and token consumption, violating Invariant 1 and Invariant 3.
4. **The GraphRAG Seeding Gap:** Typical GraphRAG architectures seed random walks either from raw text chunks or single uncalibrated entity matches. Without carrying forward the continuous candidate probability distribution, retrieval cannot distinguish high-confidence univalent terms from ambiguous multi-candidate terms.

### Decision

We formalize **Probabilistic Candidate Priors and Topological Disambiguation** as a core architectural substrate spanning ingestion, storage, retrieval, and W3C semantic alignment.

```
                      Query Mentions: { a_1, a_2, ... }
                                      │
                                      ▼
             Compiled Cython InMemoryLookupKB Candidate Lookup
                                      │
                      ┌───────────────┴───────────────┐
                      ▼                               ▼
            P(e_1 | a_1) = 0.85             P(e_2 | a_1) = 0.15
            (ML Transformer)                (Electrical Transformer)
                      │                               │
                      └───────────────┬───────────────┘
                                      ▼
                        Probabilistic Seed Vector (p0)
                 p0[e] ∝ sum_a P(e | a) * sim(q, e)
                                      │
                                      ▼
              Topological Disambiguation via Power Iteration PPR
                     p^(t+1) = α * P^T * p^(t) + (1 - α) * p0
             (Contextual neighbors reinforce ML Transformer;
              isolated Electrical Transformer mass dissipates)
                                      │
                                      ▼
                       Converged Distribution (p*)
                                      │
                                      ▼
                        Bayesian Evidence Chunk Scoring
              Score(c) = [ sum_e P(e|a)*p*[e] + p*[c] ] * sim(q, c)
```

#### 1. In-Process Cython Candidate Distribution via `InMemoryLookupKB`
- Instead of procedural dictionaries or premature discretization, candidate distributions are maintained in-process via spaCy's native compiled C/Cython `InMemoryLookupKB`.
- Each surface alias $a$ maps to candidate canonical identities $e \in \text{cands}(a)$ with calibrated prior probabilities $P(e \mid a) \in (0, 1]$ satisfying unit sum:
  $$\sum_{e \in \text{cands}(a)} P(e \mid a) = 1.0$$
- **Database Synchronization:** The `sync_kb_from_db(kb, conn)` routine synchronizes canonical entities from `leaf_nodes`, base predicates from `config.BASE_PREDICATES`, and active aliases from `aliases`. Scores for shared aliases are grouped and normalized:
  $$P(e_i \mid a) = \frac{\text{score}(a, e_i)}{\sum_{k} \text{score}(a, e_k)}$$
- **Cython Execution:** Calling `kb.get_alias_candidates(alias)` returns candidate structs (`c.entity_`, `c.prior_prob`) directly in compiled C, executing in $< 5 \ \mu\text{s}$.

#### 2. Probabilistic Seed Personalization Vector ($\mathbf{p}_0$)
The personalized seed vector $\mathbf{p}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{|V|}$ injects query intent into the graph across concepts, dense vector chunk hits, and lexical BM25 matches:
$$\mathbf{p}_0 = \beta_1 \cdot \mathbf{s}_{\text{concepts}} + \beta_2 \cdot \mathbf{s}_{\text{chunks\_vec}} + \beta_3 \cdot \mathbf{s}_{\text{chunks\_fts}}$$
where $\beta_1 = 0.60$, $\beta_2 = 0.20$, $\beta_3 = 0.20$, and $\sum_u \mathbf{p}_0[u] = 1.0$.

For each extracted query mention $a \in \text{mentions}(q)$, the concept canopy seed assigns probability mass proportional to candidate prior probability modulated by query-concept dense vector similarity:
$$\mathbf{s}_{\text{concepts}}[e] \propto \sum_{a \in \text{mentions}(q)} P(e \mid a) \cdot \max\left(0.0, \, \text{sim}(q, e)\right)$$

If EmbeddingGemma is offline ($\mathbf{q} = \mathbf{0}$):
$$\mathbf{s}_{\text{concepts}}[e] \propto \sum_{a \in \text{mentions}(q)} P(e \mid a)$$
ensuring continuous mathematical degradation without discrete failure modes.

#### 3. Topological Disambiguation via Spectral Random Walk Diffusion
Personalized PageRank power iteration propagates probability mass across the transposed row-stochastic transition matrix $\mathbf{P}^T$:
$$\mathbf{p}^{(t+1)} = \alpha \left( \mathbf{P}^T \mathbf{p}^{(t)} + m_{\text{dangling}} \mathbf{p}_0 \right) + (1 - \alpha) \mathbf{p}_0$$
where $\alpha = 0.85$, $30$ iterations, and conductance asymmetry enforces $1.0$ downward broadcast and $0.2$ upward abstraction with in-degree hub dampening $\gamma = 0.5$.

**Mathematical Mechanism of Topological Disambiguation:**
Let query $q$ contain polysemous mention $a$ with candidates $\{e_1, e_2\}$ and unambiguous contextual term $b$ linked to $e_3$.
- In the initial seed vector $\mathbf{p}_0$, both $e_1$ and $e_2$ receive positive mass: $\mathbf{p}_0[e_1] = P(e_1 \mid a) \cdot \text{sim}(q, e_1)$ and $\mathbf{p}_0[e_2] = P(e_2 \mid a) \cdot \text{sim}(q, e_2)$.
- In the knowledge graph, $e_1$ and $e_3$ belong to the same semantic cluster (e.g. shared neighbors, mutual wikilinks, common `manifests_in` chunks), whereas $e_2$ belongs to a disjoint cluster.
- During power iteration diffusion, the probability flow circulating between $e_1$ and $e_3$ constructively interferes:
  $$\mathbf{p}^*[e_1] \approx (1-\alpha) \mathbf{p}_0[e_1] + \alpha \sum_{k} P_{k, e_1} \mathbf{p}^*[k] \gg \mathbf{p}^*[e_2]$$
- The spurious candidate $e_2$, lacking topological support from the query's other seeds, sheds its mass into the broader graph or dangling sinks.
- Consequently, **disambiguation occurs as an emergent topological property of the random walk**, requiring zero runtime LLM calls.

#### 4. Bayesian Evidence Chunk Scoring
Winning candidate chunks $c \in \text{chunks}$ are scored by fusing direct chunk relevance with the stationary probability of the entities that manifest within them:
$$\text{Score}(c) = \left( \sum_{e \in \text{entities}(c)} P(e \mid a) \cdot \mathbf{p}^*[e] + \mathbf{p}^*[c] \right) \cdot \text{sim}(q, c)$$
Where:
- $P(e \mid a)$ is the candidate prior probability of mention $a$ linking to entity $e$ within chunk $c$.
- $\mathbf{p}^*[e]$ is the converged stationary PPR mass of entity $e$.
- $\mathbf{p}^*[c]$ is the stationary PPR mass accumulated directly by chunk $c$ via graph diffusion.
- $\text{sim}(q, c) = \mathbf{q} \cdot \mathbf{c}$ is the bi-encoder dot-product similarity (or $1.0$ when offline).

This guarantees that chunks containing topologically corroborated entities receive compound rank elevation, while chunks containing spurious polysemous candidates are ranked down.

#### 5. W3C Semantic Alignment & Non-Punning Compliance
- **SKOS Thesaurus Alignment:**
  - Fractional candidate aliases map directly to `skos:altLabel` on the target `skos:Concept`:
    ```turtle
    concepts:transformer-neural-network a skos:Concept ;
        skos:prefLabel "Transformer Neural Network"@en ;
        skos:altLabel "Transformer"@en .
    ```
  - Prior probability $P(e \mid a)$ is recorded as an epistemic qualifier in the relational quad substrate (`aliases.score = 0.85`).
- **OWL 2 Identity:**
  - Distinct concepts maintain distinct URIs (`owl:differentFrom`).
  - Active single-candidate canonical merges project to `owl:sameAs`.
- **Non-Punning Invariant (ADR-008, ADR-009):**
  - Epistemic thesaurus concepts (`skos:Concept`) and domain classes (`owl:Class`) remain strictly partitioned; an entity URI is never punned across both metamodeling layers.

### Mathematical Properties & Convergence

1. **Unit-Sum Conservation:** Because $\sum_{e} P(e \mid a) = 1.0$ for each mention $a$, the seed projection satisfies $\|\mathbf{p}_0\|_1 = 1.0$.
2. **Perron-Frobenius Uniqueness:** Since $\mathbf{P}$ is row-stochastic and $\alpha = 0.85 \in (0, 1)$, the transition operator $\mathbf{M} = \alpha \mathbf{P}^T + (1-\alpha) \mathbf{p}_0 \mathbf{1}^T$ is a contraction mapping in the $L_1$ norm. By the Banach Fixed Point Theorem, the power iteration converges to a unique stationary distribution $\mathbf{p}^*$ at geometric rate $\mathcal{O}(\alpha^t)$.
3. **Monotonicity under Topological Support:** If candidate $e_1$ has a higher density of directed walks connecting to other seed nodes than candidate $e_2$, then $\mathbf{p}^*[e_1] / \mathbf{p}_0[e_1] > \mathbf{p}^*[e_2] / \mathbf{p}_0[e_2]$, ensuring that topological consensus strictly enhances correct candidate probability.

---

### Consequences & Trade-offs

- **Positive:** Eliminates premature "winner-take-all" discretization during ingestion, preserving vital semantic ambiguity for downstream resolution.
- **Positive:** Achieves emergent entity disambiguation through global graph topology without expensive LLM pairwise comparisons (0 LLM calls during retrieval).
- **Positive:** Microsecond candidate lookup powered by compiled Cython `InMemoryLookupKB`.
- **Positive:** Seamlessly compatible with offline execution: continuous math ensures graceful degradation to lexical priors when embeddings are unavailable.
- **Positive:** Full auditability in SQLite: `aliases.score` stores calibrated prior probabilities accessible to SQL views and W3C serializers.
- **Trade-off:** In extremely sparse graphs (e.g. fewer than 5 notes with no shared entities), topological support is weak, meaning disambiguation relies primarily on the initial prior $P(e \mid a)$ and dense vector similarity.
